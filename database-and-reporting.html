Reporting without the expensive joins.

01

The constraint

The education platform served teachers, students, schools, and administrators, alongside CRM and LMS functionality. Reporting queries relied on heavy aggregations, sometimes spanning seven or eight joins.

02

What I owned

I owned the database design and the reporting optimization approach: materialized views, indexes, and a Redis caching layer to protect the database from repeated reads.

03

Precompute repeated work

Materialized views centralized expensive aggregation work so reporting could query prepared results. Relevant product actions, including lesson completion, quizzes, and reviews, triggered updates to those views.

04

Keep cached results current

Redis reduced repeated database reads. Relevant updates invalidated cache entries, while TTL expiry provided an additional expiration mechanism. Indexes supported the remaining query paths.

05

The measured result

CRM database latency fell from approximately 200 ms to 30–40 ms. This is the reported result of the combined optimization work, not an isolated benchmark of any single component.

06

The engineering tradeoff

Precomputed views and caching introduce freshness and invalidation responsibilities. Connecting refreshes to product events made those responsibilities part of the application workflow.
